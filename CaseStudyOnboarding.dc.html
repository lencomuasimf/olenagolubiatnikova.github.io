Onboarding adapting to every user

One flow that meets landlords wherever they are, regardless of digital literacy.

The problem

To enable rent collection, landlords must complete three required steps. Each step is located in a different part of the platform and has its own notifications and status updates.

THE COST OF THAT
Slow marketplace growth
Complicated onboarding kept users from activating.
Heavy support load
Customer Ops spent significant time walking users through the steps.
Lost bookings
A critical step, adding a bank account, interrupted the booking flow and caused conversion to drop at the worst possible moment.

How I approached it

01
Mapped the current onboarding
Onboarding tasks were scattered everywhere and so were the notifications about them.
02
Looked at the data
Data analysis showed that the step of listing connection to the bank account had the lowest completion, largely because users had no preview of their actions.
03
User interviews + feedback from Customer Ops
Talking to Customer Ops highlighted the highest failing group of users: those with lower digital literacy who rely on SMS. They have limited access to the banners, notifications and in-platform messaging, so the current experience essentially leaves them uninformed.

Top pain points

Interrupted flow
Booking flow interruptions were causing conversion drop.
Banner blindness
Requirements were communicated through banners. With several requirements at once, the screen was showing competing banners and only 14% of users clicked the banner CTA.
“Just tell me what to do.”
Some tasks had long banner descriptions; others (like notification bubbles) had none. Inconsistent guidance plus too many steps created confusion and cognitive load.
Limited onboarding reach
Our onboarding relied solely on in-platform notifications, leaving users who rely on SMS out of the onboarding experience.

Solving it

Ideate, then prioritise

Before the team workshop, I drafted ideas mapped to each pain point, for example:

  • A Customer Ops handbook so support could guide users consistently.
  • A floating button / updated bubble showing remaining tasks.
  • One home page for all tasks: not just for onboarding, but future ones too (DAC7 compliance, deposit collection, and more).

I ran a workshop with the wider team to understand the technical complexity and decide what to ship first and what to do later.

The strategy

Ship something small and cost-effective fast, while building the bigger fix in parallel.

QUICK WIN
The onboarding handbook.
LONG-TERM
One flow for every situation → Monoflow. Entry points not only through the platform, but also via SMS and email.

Prototype and test

The first prototype was a mess: tasks in mismatched styles (Stripe onboarding looked nothing like listing connection), and edge cases surfaced all at once. Testing made one thing clear: naming matters, and people need plain language to be able to understand each step.

Iterate and map entry points

After the first round I significantly improved the copy and redesigned the screens for a seamless experience. Mobile was prioritised. Then I mapped entry points: not just the SMS link, but a permanent home page on the dashboard with a notification bubble. One place to manage every operational task.

What went live

1
Dynamic Monoflow
Decides which tasks to show, and when.
2
In-platform entry points
Dashboard home + bubble. No more banners!
3
Out-of-platform entry points
SMS onboarding link + email campaign.

Impact

+7%
Bank accounts onboarded
In the first week after launching Monoflow and the dashboard entry point.
↑
Activation rate
A single guided flow raised activation rate across the marketplace.
↑
More bookings
Fewer interruptions in the booking flow improved conversion.

Fewer support requests related to onboarding.

Better UX across the product: Monoflow rebuilt multiple scattered forms, so they’re now consistent, accessible, and have better usability.
